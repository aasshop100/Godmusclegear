BPC-157: The Complete Guide to
Dosage, Benefits & Results

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound 157) is a synthetic peptide derived from a protein found in human gastric juice. It has become one of the most talked-about compounds in the recovery and performance enhancement world — and for good reason. Research suggests it accelerates healing in tendons, muscles, ligaments and even gut tissue at a remarkable rate.

This complete guide covers everything you need to know: what BPC-157 actually does, the correct dosage, how to reconstitute it, and what results to realistically expect.

What is BPC-157?

BPC-157 is a pentadecapeptide — a chain of 15 amino acids (Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val). It was first isolated from human gastric juice and has shown remarkable regenerative properties across numerous animal studies. It promotes angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation), accelerates collagen synthesis and modulates growth hormone receptors — all of which contribute to dramatically faster healing.

Key Benefits of BPC-157

  • Tendon & Ligament Healing — accelerates recovery from tears, strains and overuse injuries
  • Muscle Repair — speeds up recovery from muscle tears and DOMS
  • Gut Health — heals gastric ulcers, leaky gut and inflammatory bowel conditions
  • Joint Protection — reduces inflammation and pain in damaged joints
  • Neuroprotection — some evidence for nerve regeneration and brain injury recovery
  • Anti-inflammatory — systemically reduces inflammation markers

💡 BPC-157 is particularly popular among athletes using anabolic steroids, as heavy training loads and compounds like Trenbolone can place significant stress on tendons and connective tissue.

BPC-157 Dosage Protocol

Standard Research Dosage

200–500mcg per day, injected subcutaneously (under the skin) or intramuscularly, ideally near the site of injury. Split into two injections of 250mcg morning and evening for best results.

Cycle Length

4–6 weeks for acute injury recovery. Some users run 8–12 weeks for chronic issues. A 4-week break between cycles is recommended.

How to Reconstitute BPC-157

BPC-157 comes as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder in a vial, typically 5mg per vial. You'll need bacteriostatic water (BAC water) to reconstitute it.

  1. Wipe the rubber stopper of both the BPC-157 vial and BAC water vial with an alcohol swab
  2. Draw 1–2mL of BAC water into a syringe
  3. Slowly inject the BAC water into the BPC-157 vial — let it run down the side of the vial, do not spray directly onto the powder
  4. Gently swirl (do not shake) until the powder fully dissolves
  5. Store reconstituted peptide in the refrigerator (2–8°C) — use within 30 days

Dosage calculation: If you add 2mL of BAC water to a 5mg vial, each 0.1mL (10 units on an insulin syringe) contains 250mcg. Use our Peptide Calculator to determine your exact draw volume.

Administration: Subcutaneous vs Intramuscular

Subcutaneous (SubQ): Inject into the fatty layer just under the skin, typically in the abdomen. Easy, relatively painless, and the most common method for systemic effects.

Intramuscular (IM): Inject directly into or near the injured area. Preferred when targeting a specific injury site — for example, injecting near a damaged tendon in the shoulder or knee.

What Results Can You Expect?

Most users report noticeable reductions in pain and inflammation within the first 1–2 weeks. Tendon and ligament injuries that normally take months to heal have been reported to recover significantly faster — sometimes 50–70% faster than without BPC-157.

For gut issues like gastric ulcers or GERD, many users notice improvement within the first week of oral or injectable use.

Side Effects

BPC-157 has an excellent safety profile in research. The most commonly reported side effects are mild and include nausea (more common with oral dosing), dizziness and temporary fatigue. No significant adverse effects have been reported at standard research doses.
